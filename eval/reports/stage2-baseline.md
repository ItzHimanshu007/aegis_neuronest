## Near-miss negatives

The page carries 14 deliberate near-misses (checksum-invalid Aadhaar, Luhn-invalid
card, PAN with a bad holder-type character, IFSC without the literal 0, phone starting
with 5, prices, years, timestamps, version strings, a high-entropy string with no
key/secret label, and an email whose first domain label looks like a UPI PSP handle).
The **rules** reject every one of them: the unit-test false-positive corpus reports
**0 false positives across 18 benign strings** (`pnpm test`, "false-positive corpus").

The **cascade** deliberately does not. Several of these near-misses sit in blocks whose
own label names the category — "Aadhaar-shaped but checksum-invalid: 2345 6789 0128" —
and the labelled-value fallback redacts a labelled value whether or not a rule matched
it. That is why AADHAAR shows 9 detections against 4 annotated values. This is the
intended trade: a label that says "Aadhaar" is stronger evidence than a checksum that
fails, and over-redacting a decoy costs the planner nothing, while under-redacting a
real number that happens to be mistyped costs the user their identity number. The
honest reading of the precision column is therefore "recall at a deliberately
fail-closed operating point", not "38% of AADHAAR hits are wrong".

## Known gaps at this baseline

- Values rendered into a `<canvas>` are invisible to a DOM-only cascade; the canvas is
  masked wholesale as `UNSCANNED_MEDIA` instead (fail-closed). OCR lands in Stage 7.
- Names and addresses in free prose with no nearby label are not detected — that is
  exactly what the Stage 7 NER hook is for.
- Anything below the fold is not in the capture at all, so a single observation only
  ever covers the current viewport. This run scrolls through every section to
  compensate.
